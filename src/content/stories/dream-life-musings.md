My dream life is one of good health, loving relationships, meaningful achievement, financial freedom, contribution, and inner peace. Not without ambition, but an ambition that comes from a good purpose rather than greed, comparison, fear or the need to prove myself.

I want to remain physically strong, energetic, and mentally alert. I want to exercise every day, eat sensibly, sleep peacefully, and enjoy long, unhurried walks in the morning with friends and with Tutu, my loving wife, in the evening. I want us to have the time and health to enjoy one another’s company, our family, close friends, music, travel, simple conversations, and even the pleasure of sometimes doing absolutely nothing.

I want Adya and Vaibhav to build successful and fulfilling lives of their own. I hope they become financially and emotionally independent, confident in their abilities, and guided by good values. I want to remain close to them—not by controlling their choices, but by being a source of unconditional love, honest guidance, and quiet strength whenever they need me.

I want enough wealth that I never have to depend on anyone or compromise my principles for money. At the same time, I do not want wealth merely for possessions or status. I want it to give me freedom, security, and the ability to support good causes, create opportunities, and improve the lives of people around me.

Professionally, I want to build ZENOJAS as one of India’s most respected and successful asset management companies. I want to bring together exceptional quant specialists, technical analysts, value investors, macroeconomic thinkers, technologists, and risk managers. Its culture should be built on independent thinking, intellectual honesty, disciplined research, patience, transparency, downside protection, and the willingness to review decisions without ego.

I want to use my experience, persistence, curiosity, and continuing interest in investment management and AI to ask the right questions, develop talented people, and make sound long-term decisions. My greatest professional achievement would not merely be the size of the company, but the creation of an institution that earns investors’ trust, creates outstanding careers, and continues to succeed even without my daily involvement.

Education helped transform my life, so I would also like to give back on this front: to be a teacher, helping people learn more easily, and to enable children in rural areas or with meagre means to have access to the best education, largely online but of great quality. The idea is to empower them with the learning so that they can grow and contribute to the development of this country.

Internally, I want greater mastery over my desires. Today I feel that my decisions and reactions are appropriate: even when the inner thought is not right, I am able to take the right action without being fully influenced by anger or fear, and they no longer govern my decisions. But what I am looking for in the dream life is more purity and simplicity in the thoughts themselves — thoughts that do not arise from greed, ego or anger. This does not mean that fear, anger, or temptation will never arise; it means that they keep reducing until they almost stop, and the mind naturally produces good, optimistic thoughts, filled with the well-being of the people around me. I want to forgive more easily, complain less, and focus my energy on finding solutions and resolving any situation rather than complicating it with a more egoistic thought process.

I want to remain relevant without constantly needing recognition. I want to have more gratitude for what God has kindly and lovingly given to me. If I am able to help others, the mind should expect nothing in return, not even gratitude. I want to lead without dominating, succeed without arrogance, and enjoy the happiness and progress of others without comparison. I want my inner thoughts to become cleaner, kinder, and more peaceful.

At the end of my life, I would like to feel that I loved my family and friends deeply, took care of my health, built something meaningful, remained eager to learn, helped people during the journey, used wealth responsibly, and gradually became a calmer and better human being.

## A typical day in my dream life

I wake up naturally and begin the morning without immediately looking at my phone. I spend a few quiet minutes in gratitude, prayer, meditation, or simply observing my thoughts.

I exercise for strength and mobility and go for a walk with friends, followed by a relaxed breakfast. I begin my work calmly, knowing the two or three important matters that deserve my attention that day.

The most focused part of my morning is devoted to investment thinking, understanding markets and the economy, reviewing important research, or discussing ideas with the investment team. I concentrate on the decisions where my experience genuinely adds value rather than becoming involved in every operational detail.

The firm is professionally managed. Part of my day is spent mentoring talented younger colleagues, encouraging disagreement, asking thoughtful questions, and ensuring that risk, ethics, and investors’ interests remain central to the company. I work hard to manage the money responsibly, but I have control over my time, and I enjoy doing this work, identifying great investment opportunities and creating wealth for our investors.

I eat lunch without hurry, with colleagues who are friends as well, or sometimes with family, and preserve some unstructured time in the afternoon for reading, learning, thinking, a short walk or simply being still. I continue studying subjects that interest me—investment management, economics, technology, AI, history, psychology, and spirituality—not because I need another qualification, but because learning keeps me alive and curious. I want to wind up by 4 pm and have some unscheduled time with no worthy activity at all — a nap, some music, a film, a swim.

Every evening, Tutu and I take a long walk together. We talk, observe the world around us, sometimes plan, sometimes laugh, and sometimes simply walk quietly. The evening belongs mainly to family and friends. Meals are unhurried, phones are kept away, and my relationship with Adya and Vaibhav is warm and supportive while respecting their independence.

Before sleeping, I want to reflect briefly: Did I take care of my health? Did I give enough time and affection to my family? Did I create something useful? Did I help someone? Did fear, greed, anger, or ego control any of my decisions? What am I grateful for today?

I then sleep with the satisfaction that the day did not have to be extraordinary to be meaningful. I was healthy, useful, loving, curious, generous, and peaceful—and that itself was a successful day.
